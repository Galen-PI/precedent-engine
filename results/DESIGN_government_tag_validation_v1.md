# DESIGN 2026-10-23 (committed BEFORE any step runs): automatic validation of the government-decision tags
GOAL. Tags usable as threads with NO per-decision human review. A tagger cannot certify itself, so people appear only at CALIBRATION (a fixed small set, once per vocabulary version) and in a tiny periodic AUDIT; every other check runs by itself. This note fixes the build order, the controls, the thresholds and the stop rules in advance.
STATE AT WRITING. 3,035 relevant decisions tagged under vocabulary v1 (prompt v1.2): 2,992 usable, 12,149 tags (persistence 2,992, scope 2,785, sector 1,558, theme 4,814). Two independent runs on 2,471 decisions agree: scope 91.3%, persistence 97.8%, theme sets identical 76.5% and sharing a theme 97.0%, sector sets (non-empty) identical 73.9% and sharing a sector 80.9%. Tiers (results via the view government_decision_tags_tiered): A 4,812 (39.6%), B 1,690 (13.9%), C 5,647 (46.5%). The registered 150 + 100 hand-read sample is frozen (results/tag_review_v1_*); no verdict has been written.
PRINCIPLES. (1) Trust is COMPUTED from evidence, never asserted. (2) Evidence is independent of the tagger wherever it can be (different prompt, different information, different data). (3) Every automatic check has a measured error rate, estimated with controls that need no reader. (4) The tag table is never edited by hand and v1 is immutable: any rule change is a new version with its own registration. (5) Nothing is bulk-confirmed: a gate decides, and its thresholds are fixed here.
THE EVIDENCE STACK. L0 mechanical: quote found in the text shown, vocabulary, validator rules (built). L1 self-consistency: agreement between repeat runs (built for 2,471 decisions). L2 independent verifier: a differently worded, adversarial model check on each tag, blind to the tagger's confidence, the tier and the repeat status (to build). L3 external anchors: for laws, Congress's human-curated policy area (used, 76-83% agreement with the model's themes) and legislative subject terms, given to the verifier as extra information (to pilot, then build). L4 outcome: do tagged sectors react more than other sectors on the decision's date (own registration, no reader). L5 calibration and audit: a fixed small human or reader set that turns the other evidence into a precision per tier.
BUILD ORDER (each step: what, acceptance, fallback). 
 1. COMPLETE THE REPEAT RUN: tag the decisions that have no v1_rerun run (about 564) with --label v1_rerun. Acceptance: every decision has two runs; agreement on the new 564 within 3 points of the earlier figures (scope 91%, persistence 98%). Fallback: if lower by more than 5 points, investigate the difference in the new decisions before using stability for them.
 2. CRS SUBJECT TERMS (pilot 3 laws, read the raw response, then all laws): table of policy area and legislative subjects per law. Acceptance: at least 90% of laws have at least one subject term. Fallback: policy area only.
 3. VERIFIER PASS (own script, own parser, own table of verdicts; verdict supports | does_not_support | unsure plus a one-line reason). Themes and sectors are verified at tag level from the quote, about 600 characters of context, the title, the theme or sector gloss and, for laws, the CRS subjects; scope and persistence are verified at decision level from the title and the first 1,500 characters. CONTROLS (no reader): COUNTERFEIT TAGS: a seeded 300 theme and 300 sector tags have their label swapped for a different label of the same type that the decision does not already carry, the quote kept, and are submitted mixed with the real tags under hashed ids. FALSE-ACCEPT RATE fa = share of counterfeits marked supports; estimated precision of verifier-accepted real tags = (accept_rate_real - fa) / (1 - fa) with a bootstrap interval over decisions, reported as OPTIMISTIC because swapped labels are easier to reject than subtle errors. Acceptance: output parseable at least 98%; fa at most 20%. Fallback: if fa exceeds 25% the verifier is not independent enough to carry weight and calibration falls back to a larger human or reader sample.
 4. TIERS v2 (below), computed in a view, thresholds fixed now.
 5. MARKET TEST (own pre-registration BEFORE it runs): for decisions with sector tags, the tagged sector's excess movement on the decision date and the next day against the untagged sectors (the paired design, baseline and exclusions of the Fed-day sector tests), split by tier. No reader needed. Whatever the result, a tag that does not move prices does not help the lane, and one that does is useful.
 6. CALIBRATION READ: the frozen random sample (150 decisions) read by Claude, plus a human on as many as the human can read (a 10-decision minimum on scope and themes is worth reporting); contested tags (tagger and verifier disagree) are read in addition and reported separately. Output: precision per tier with Wilson and decision-bootstrap intervals; a tier EARNS USE when its precision meets the registered bar (themes at least 85%, sectors at least 80% with a Wilson lower bound of at least 70%).
 7. FINER BUCKETS as CHILDREN of verified parents (vocabulary v2): constrained to their parent, quote required, own registration, validated with the same stack (verifier, counterfeits, a small read). v1 is never changed. Candidate splits (to be chosen by Galen): budget_appropriations; government_operations; Industrials; Health Care; Financials; trade_tariffs.
TIERS v2 (definitions fixed now). A+ = tier A (verified quote, confidence medium or high, the Public Law text, full or partial text, present in BOTH runs) AND the verifier says supports. A = tier A without verifier support (reported, never used as primary). B = tier B or A with verifier unsure. C = everything else, or verifier does_not_support. USE: primary analyses use A+ only; A and B are sensitivity checks; C is never used except to describe the data. A tag cannot be promoted by hand.
MONITORING AND STOP RULES. CANARIES: 100 decisions fixed from the frozen sample are re-tagged every quarter and whenever the model version changes; pause new tagging and investigate if scope agreement with the frozen v1 tags falls below 88% or theme overlap below 93% (the test-retest figures minus a margin). AUDIT: 30 random tier A+ tags per quarter are read; PAUSE the lane (not discard) if at least 6 of 30 theme tags or at least 10 of 30 sector tags are wrong. Version changes of the model, the prompt, the vocabulary or the validator rules each require a new version label and a new calibration.
HUMAN ROLES. Define the vocabulary and the rubric; read or oversee the calibration set once per version; read the quarterly audit; adjudicate the contested queue if it is small. A pipeline with no person anywhere cannot detect that it is wrong; this design makes the person's work a fixed small cost instead of a per-decision cost.
PREDICTIONS (point and probability). Step 1: agreement on the new 564 within 3 points of the earlier figures 80%. Step 2: at least 90% of laws have a subject term 70%. Step 3: counterfeit false-accept at most 20% 80%, at most 10% 60%; the verifier supports at least 85% of tier A theme tags 70% and at least 70% of tier A sector tags 65%; parseable at least 98% 85%. Tiers: A+ is at least 30% of all tags 60%. Step 5: tier A+ sector tags show a larger excess than tier C sector tags 60%, a statistically significant tier A+ effect on its own 25%. Step 6: tier A+ theme precision at least 92% 60%, sector precision at least 85% 50%. Estimated cost of steps 1 to 3: about two to three tagging runs of compute, no hand-reading.
WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE PLAN. A verifier that cannot reject counterfeits (fa above 25%): calibration becomes reader-based and larger. A market test with no tier A+ effect: the sector lane is described, not used, and the theme and scope buckets carry the government lane. Repeat agreement on the new decisions more than 5 points lower: the pipeline is not stationary and stability is not used for them. A calibration miss against the bar: the registered one prompt revision applies, as a new version.
NOT IN THIS DESIGN. The earlier relevance screen's re-run on full text (the tagging already measured it: at least 17% of screened-relevant decisions are confidently not company relevant); the enrolled-bill text upgrade for pre-2005 laws (optional); any change to v1.
ADDENDUM 2026-10-26 (written BEFORE the verifier is built): STRATIFIED FALSE-ACCEPT. CRS subject terms are loaded for all 2,176 relevant laws: median 17 terms per law (p10 5, p25 8, p75 52, p90 191.5, max 1,964), only 2 laws with the policy area alone. RISK: on laws with hundreds of terms, almost any theme or sector tag finds some supporting term, which would inflate the verifier's accept rate and make the check look better than it is. RULE: the counterfeit false-accept rate is reported separately by law size, in three strata by terms per law: up to 8, 9 to 52, more than 52 (orders and any decision without subject terms form their own group, evaluated without subject terms). Acceptance is unchanged at 20% or lower overall, and now ALSO at 20% or lower in each stratum that holds at least 50 counterfeits. A stratum above 25% has the subject terms REMOVED from the verifier's input for laws of that size (the verifier then works from the quote, the context and the label definition alone there), is re-measured the same way, and its accepts count toward tier A+ only if it then meets the 20% bar. PREDICTIONS: the false-accept rate in the more-than-52 stratum is higher than in the up-to-8 stratum 70%; the more-than-52 stratum exceeds 20% 40%; at least one stratum exceeds 25% 30%.
NOTE ON THE POLICY-AREA MATCH (2,176 of 2,176 = 100.0%): the stored policy area and the one fetched now very probably come from the same Congress.gov data via different endpoints, so the match shows that every law was joined to the RIGHT BILL (an identifier-integrity check), NOT that the subject terms validate the tagger. Whether the terms help the verifier is tested by the counterfeits above.
