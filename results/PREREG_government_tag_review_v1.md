# PRE-REGISTRATION 2026-10-23 (committed BEFORE the sample is drawn): the hand-read validity test of the government-decision tags, vocabulary v1, prompt v1.2
SCRIPT: scripts/government_decisions/build_tag_review_sheet.py (read-only on the database; it only writes files under results/). BAR (from results/government_tag_vocabulary_v1.md): SECTOR-tag precision at least 80% with a Wilson lower bound of at least 70%, and THEME-tag precision at least 85%; otherwise the prompt is revised once and the read repeated on a fresh sample; nothing is bulk-confirmed.
POPULATION: decisions whose v1 run status is ok or partial (2,992 of 3,035; the 43 invalid ones have no tags and are reported separately).
PRIMARY SAMPLE (150): strata = decision type (enacted_law, executive_order) x era of decision_date (<=2004, 2005-2014, >=2015); proportional allocation with a minimum of 15 per stratum, adjusted so the total is exactly 150; within a stratum a seeded shuffle (seed 20261023), first n taken. LOW-CONFIDENCE SAMPLE (100): decisions NOT in the primary sample whose tags carry confidence low, seeded (seed + 1). INDEPENDENT READ: a seeded random 30 of the primary 150 (seed + 2) are read by Galen BEFORE he sees any verdict of Claude's; Claude reads all 250. The reading order is a seeded shuffle (not by stratum).
BLINDING: the sheets do NOT show whether a tag also appeared in the repeat run (label v1_rerun) or which decisions are in the independent subset; those live in results/tag_review_v1_attributes.csv and the manifest and are merged only AFTER the verdicts are written.
RUBRIC (the unit is a TAG; verdicts are correct | wrong | unsure). THEME: correct if the quote is in the text and the theme's gloss (in the prompt) applies to what the quote describes, not merely a shared word. SECTOR: correct if the quote names or clearly describes that sector's own activity AND the decision plausibly changes the economics of that sector's companies in the stated direction (mixed is accepted when both effects are plausible). SCOPE: correct given the whole stored text and the definitions (unsure if the shown text is too thin to judge). PERSISTENCE: correct under the prompt's rule. For every sector_specific decision with no sector tag the reader notes whether an obvious sector was omitted (descriptive recall check).
ANALYSIS (fixed now): precision = correct / (correct + wrong), unsure excluded and also reported as wrong in a sensitivity line; Wilson interval on tags AND a bootstrap over DECISIONS (tags within a decision are correlated, so the tag-level interval is too narrow); per-bucket-type results; descriptive splits by stability (tag present in the repeat run or not), confidence, text kind (full, partial, thin), era and type; the independent-read agreement (percent agreement per bucket type on the 30); the low-confidence sample reported separately and never pooled into the bar.
PREDICTIONS (point and range): theme precision 90% (82-95); sector precision 78% (62-90); scope accuracy 82% (72-90); persistence accuracy 85% (75-92); P(sector bar met) 40%; P(theme bar met) 65%; sector tags present in BOTH runs are at least 5 points more precise than those in one run only 65%; unsure at most 10% of tags 70%; low-confidence sample precision at least 10 points below the primary 65%; Claude-Galen agreement on the 30 at least 85% per bucket type 65%.
LIMITS: the reader judges the stored text (a partial text shows only its start plus headings); the sample is of decisions the earlier title-only relevance screen called relevant; Claude is both the tagger's family and a reader (mitigated by the independent human read and the quote check); one reading of 250 decisions is not repeated.
