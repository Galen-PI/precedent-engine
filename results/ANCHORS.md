# ANCHORS: published magnitudes and standard methods to check a registration against (created 2026-10-26)
HOW TO USE. Every numeric prediction in a registration carries an anchor label (SCRIPTS.md addendum 24): DATA, LITERATURE (cite the line below), or NONE (then the range is wide). After a result, compare it with the anchor FIRST: a result far from a published magnitude is a reason to look for a data problem before claiming a finding.
VERIFIED BY WEB SEARCH ON 2026-10-26 (abstracts or summaries read; the full papers were not):
1. PRE-FOMC DRIFT. Lucca and Moench, "The Pre-FOMC Announcement Drift", Journal of Finance 70(1), 2015: the S&P 500 rose on average 49 basis points in the 24 hours before scheduled FOMC announcements since 1994 (intraday window). https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr512.pdf . Our replication: +0.449% two-day excess return, 1994-2011 (t +3.03). A later extension (secondary summary only, https://trademachine.substack.com/p/pre-fomc-announcement-drift; READ THE PAPER BEFORE REGISTERING) reports the drift concentrated in announcements followed by press conferences and starting at the open the day before; press conferences began in April 2011, our sample split. Lead, not a finding: split post-2011 meetings by press conference.
2. PRICE CLUSTERING AFTER DECIMALIZATION. Ikenberry and Weston, "Clustering in US Stock Prices after Decimalisation", European Financial Management 14(1), 2008: widespread clustering at nickels and dimes, about double what is otherwise expected, no Nasdaq-NYSE difference, better explained by a human preference for prominent numbers. https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/eufman/v14y2008i1p30-54.html . Our closes: whole dollar 3.19 (2001-06 to 2013) and 1.71 (2014-2026) times the plain-cent mean, a hierarchy dollar > half > dime > quarter > nickel (SCRIPTS.md addendum 23). A 2025 journal abstract (market not stated in the excerpt) reports more clustering for higher prices, lower turnover and lower liquidity.
3. MULTIPLE-TESTING HURDLE. Harvey, Liu and Zhu, "... and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", Review of Financial Studies 29(1), 2016: a newly discovered factor needs a t-ratio above 3.0. https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592
4. REPLICATION. Hou, Xue and Zhang, "Replicating Anomalies", Review of Financial Studies 33(5), 2020: with microcaps mitigated, 65% of 452 anomalies fail |t| >= 1.96 and 82% fail 2.78; replicated magnitudes are often much smaller than originally reported. https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i5p2019-2133..html . Reading: anomalies concentrate in microcaps (about 3% of market cap, about 61% of stocks); our universe is today's 490 large caps, so many nulls are the expected outcome.
5. OUT-OF-SAMPLE DECAY. McLean and Pontiff, "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?", Journal of Finance 71(1), 2016: 97 predictors; returns 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication; larger declines for predictors with higher in-sample returns. https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf . PLANNING RULE: expect a forward magnitude of about 0.4 to 0.75 of the in-sample one.
6. BACKTEST OVERFITTING. Bailey and Lopez de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio" (2014), https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551 ; Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting" (2015), https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253 : correct for the number of trials; testing more than 45 configurations on a five-year sample almost guarantees an in-sample Sharpe of 1 with an out-of-sample Sharpe of 0. USE: declare N trials before any combination test (the docket step 'register 5 to 8 combinations').
7. INTERVAL-FORECAST EVALUATION. Kupiec (1995) unconditional coverage; Christoffersen, "Evaluating Interval Forecasts", International Economic Review 39, 1998, 841-862 (DOI 10.2307/2527341): coverage frequency, independence of misses and their combination (conditional coverage). USE: add a descriptive Kupiec/Christoffersen analysis of the range product's monthly hit sequence AFTER the January verdicts (never in the frozen scripts).
