# DECISION 2026-10-30: day 0 stays the filing date (timing thread, item 3)

STATUS: DECIDED by Galen Pike on 2026-10-30 (his reply to the recommendation: "Your recommendation for item 3 makes the most sense"); written by the assistant. A standing rule. It creates no flag and changes no ledger row.

DECISION: for every flag, test and re-run in this project, day 0 of an event is the filing date (events.event_date), exactly as before. No re-dated day 0 is adopted. The tables sec_8k_report_dates and event_timing are DIAGNOSTIC: they may be used to describe or split a result by filing lag (td_lag, k_star) and by acceptance window (accepted_bucket), and they may be cited, but they may not move day 0 of any registered definition.

WHY (the evidence, with files):
1. Lag 0 is 8,880 of 22,385 linked events (39.7 percent). For them the acceptance window decides the reaction day: filed before the open, day 0 1.919 (day +1 1.221); during the session, day 0 1.485; after the close, day 0 1.104 and day +1 1.629. A correction would need the acceptance time, and the days 0 to 1 window already contains both reaction days. (results/g7_rerun/reaction_day_profile_results_2026-10-30.out)
2. Lag 1 is 5,658 events (25.3 percent). Day 0, the filing date, is the peak in all four groups (before the open 1.756, during the session 1.394, after the close 1.361, rolled 1.448), including filings accepted after the close, where day +1 had been predicted. The filing date is already the reaction day. Lags 0 and 1 together are 14,538 events (64.9 percent).
3. Lags 2 to 4 are 6,772 events (30.3 percent). The reaction is spread: an early reaction on the trading days after the report date (lag 2 day -1 1.285; lag 3 day -2 1.171; lag 4 days -4 and -3, 1.210 and 1.177), then a second wave with the filing (lag 2 days 0 and +1 about 1.27; lag 3 day 0 1.210; lag 4 day +1 1.128). The early reaction is robust to widening the isolation window from 5 to 14 days before the filing: all four registered conditions hold (L4 day -4 1.202, day -3 1.154; L3 day -2 1.169; L2 day -1 1.270; 90 to 93 percent of the events kept), so it is not a neighbouring-event artifact. (results/g7_rerun/reaction_day_isolation_results_2026-10-30.out) No single day captures such an event: by the lag-split model about half of the late filings carry news public at the report date and about half only at the filing (fitted, not measured).
4. Lags of 5 or more (1,075 events, 4.8 percent) cannot be re-centered: the daily data effectively start at day -4.
5. Late-filed events are not less newsy: the summed excess reaction over days -4 to +4 is about 0.7 to 0.9 for lags 2 to 4 and 0.5 to 1.5 for the same-day and lag-1 groups (a post-seen description, not a registered test).
So no single re-dated day 0 improves the dating of all events: lag 0 needs the acceptance time, lag 1 is already right, and lags 2 to 4 are mixtures.

CONSEQUENCES FOR G7 AND THE LEDGER:
- The flags already re-run (10 by the 2026-10-30 checkpoint's count) and the ones still to run use the filing date as day 0 under their original definitions. Nothing already run needs to be repeated.
- The headline burst (days 0 and 1 against days 25 to 39, 1.31 pooled) is a blend: 1.405 for same-day filings, 1.351 at lag 1, and 1.269, 1.179 and 1.099 at lags 2, 3 and 4 (lag-split result). It is not wrong under its definition, but for lags of 2 or more it understates the event's reaction, because part of it falls before day 0.
- The Tier 1 candidate event_pre_days_m4_m1 stays null: largely explained by the 8-K filing lag; a residual of +0.024 (z 1.81) is not distinguishable from zero; its January 2027 forward check is dropped (PREREG lag split, decision rule). The ledger note is written at the end of G7.

WHAT IS ALLOWED: reporting splits by lag and acceptance window in new tests. A lag-aware or reaction-day idea (for example a burst measured from the report date for late filings, or a day +1 window for filings accepted after the close) must be a NEW registered flag, with its own definition, predictions first, the stated hurdle and a forward check on the January 2027 data. Existing flag definitions are not edited because of this note.

REVISIT WHEN: (1) a registered lag-aware flag passes its forward test; (2) event_daily_abn is rebuilt with more days before the event (for example 15), so that lags of 5 or more and the true event day become visible; (3) the January 2027 data fail to reproduce the lag 0 and lag 1 peak days.

OPEN POINTS (not resolved): the lag-1 after-close mechanism (the news reaches the market during the filing day, ahead of an evening filing, although the report date is the day before) is described, not explained; condition (iii) of the profile test passed by the narrowest margin (lag 3 at a displayed 1.100); all standard errors are naive (events are treated as independent); the sealed set was part of the results and is not independent confirmation (confirmation is the January 2027 data); the profile uses absolute abnormal returns, so it shows the size of a move and not its sign.
